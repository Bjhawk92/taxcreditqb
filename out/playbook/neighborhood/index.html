Neighborhood Meeting

Uncover the concerns. Answer them.

Introduce your team and comparable developments, then use a structured Q&A process to uncover the neighborhood’s concerns. Document objections, answer questions and identify the arguments likely to emerge at approval hearings.

Intro. Track record. What this site is. Open the floor in a planned order. Answer — don’t debate. Leave a one-pager. Opposition has a playbook; you should have one too.

Objection map

ConcernWhat they meanWhat you showWhat you never say
Density / height / ‘out of scale’They are afraid the block changes and they were not asked.Context elevations. What is next door. What you already cut.‘You don’t understand urbanism.’
Property valuesThey are pricing their house in public.Comps and the restriction that keeps it residential, maintained, and privately managed.‘The data says you should be grateful.’
Traffic and parkingThey already lose the street at 5 p.m.The city’s trip gen, on-site parking, and what you will not do to their curb.‘It’s not that many cars.’
Crime / ‘those people’This is the one they will not say cleanly. You still have to answer it cleanly.Screening, management, lighting, who is on-site. Product, not a sermon.A lecture about bias from the podium.
Process / ‘railroaded’They think the deal is already done.The calendar. What is still a vote. How to comment. Your phone number.‘This is a formality.’
